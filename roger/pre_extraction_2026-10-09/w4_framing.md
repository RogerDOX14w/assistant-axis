# W4: advisor and "urging others" framing in trait descriptions

**21 real cases** in the 916 trait descriptions, plus **5 partner edits** that keep a clean pair matched: 26 files to rewrite and regenerate.  16 of the 21 are poles of clean pairs, and 6 of the pairs touched are judged axes.
**48 borderline traits** in 16 groups, all recommended to leave as they are; two milder registers (29 "emphasizing" descriptions, and output words such as "presenting information") are described at the end but not drafted.

Item W4 of the [pre-extraction work list](./work_list.md), 2026-10-09.  Read-only so far: no corpus file has been changed.

**Terms.**  *(a) advisor framing*: the trait written as answering, advising, recommending or responding to someone, or as how one's answers go, rather than as what the person is like.  *(b) urging others*: the trait written as what the persona urges on other people rather than what it does itself (Roger, 2026-09-28).  A *clean pair* is two traits whose `negative_label` fields name each other, recorded as a `pair` in their `arrangement` field; the *antonym check* (`generate_antonyms.py`) reads one trait's instructions and names its opposite, which is how a pair is confirmed.  A *partner edit* is a small change to the other pole of a pair, so that the two keep the same scope.  *V2* is the trait instruction generator adopted on 2026-10-01; "leaks into V2" quotes a current instruction that repeats the framing.  *Pair lists* are the files of judged axes in [axis_judge_experiments](../axis_judge_experiments/); "response-judged" lists are the ones whose model responses were scored by the judges.  *Unclassified* means a trait with no `arrangement` yet (W10).  Rule numbers refer to "Description-writing rules for new seeds" in [trait-pairs.md](../../.claude/rules/trait-pairs.md).  Other terms: [glossary](../../reports/trait_gap_generation/glossary.md).

## How the scan was done

I read all 916 descriptions.  The audit's regex (`answer|responses|recommend|advice|advis|expert`) now hits 55 of them: 16 are real cases or partner edits below, 10 are borderline, and the other 29 use the word in its own right (the firm answer [closure-seeking](../../data/traits/instructions/closure_seeking.json) needs, the ready answer of [glib](../../data/traits/instructions/glib.json), the doctors' advice [science-trusting](../../data/traits/instructions/science_trusting.json) follows, advising as an occupation in [social (Holland)](../../data/traits/instructions/social_holland.json), experts as the deciders in [technocratic](../../data/traits/instructions/technocratic.json)).  Seven real cases have no regex word at all ("guidance", "suggesting", "encouraging", "emphasizing the importance of", "framing solutions", "beyond what was directly asked", "streamlining explanations").  A case counts as real when the framing defines the trait (someone is answered, advised, recommended to, urged or asked, and the trait is not itself a way of treating people); it is borderline when talking to or acting on others is the trait's own content, or when the trace is a single word.

Every draft is one sentence in the corpus form: "This means ...", 21 to 31 words (checked by script), US English, no label in the opening, no partner named, the hedges dropped ("where appropriate", "potentially", "often").  Each keeps the old scope and changes the framing; where a draft does more than that, its row says so.  The 5 unpaired traits need no recheck; the 16 paired ones and the 5 partners go through the rewrite procedure below.

## Two milder registers, not drafted

- **"Emphasizing."**  29 descriptions describe the trait as what one stresses ("emphasizing teamwork", "emphasizing positive aspects"): the register of the original corpus, which was written for an assistant answering a user.  It shades into (b) only where the thing stressed is something others should do; that is why five of the 29 are real cases above ([altruistic](../../data/traits/instructions/altruistic.json), [cautious](../../data/traits/instructions/cautious.json), [collaborative](../../data/traits/instructions/collaborative.json), [meditative](../../data/traits/instructions/meditative.json), [rule-abiding](../../data/traits/instructions/rule_abiding.json)) and two are borderline ([optimistic](../../data/traits/instructions/optimistic.json), [pessimistic](../../data/traits/instructions/pessimistic.json)).
