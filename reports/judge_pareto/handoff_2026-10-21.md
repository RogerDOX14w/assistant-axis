## Summary

**What the judging is for.**  The project extracts a direction in Qwen3-32B's
activation space for every trait and role ("entity") in its corpus, and for
pairs of opposite entities it takes the difference of their vectors as an
*axis*.  To ask whether an axis means what its poles say, an LLM *judge* scores every entity in the corpus on that axis, from -3 to +3, and we correlate the scores with each entity's activations projection onto the axis.  The Spearman ρ of that correlation is the quality measure for everything below.  The judge can read three things about an entity: its description, its five persona instructions, or batches of the model's actual responses while playing it.  Responses are the most informative and by far the most expensive.

**What was done, April to May 2026.**  Roger compared judges, scoring sources
and judging parameters on the extraction of April 2026 ("8slot", 303 traits
and 281 roles, 500 responses each), chose everything by maximising mean ρ over
cohorts of axes, and fixed the results as code defaults:


| setting                                                      | value                                                                                                               | tuned on                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| judges                                                       | GPT-4.1-mini and Claude Sonnet 4 for descriptions and instructions; GPT-4.1-mini and Claude Haiku 4.5 for responses |                                    |
| description / instruction weight within one judge            | 0.499 / 0.501                                                                                                       | 33 axes, then 61                   |
| GPT / Sonnet weight, descriptions + instructions             | 0.625                                                                                                               | 35 axes                            |
| GPT / Haiku weight, responses                                | 0.525                                                                                                               | 22 axes                            |
| responses / (descriptions + instructions) in the final score | 0.80                                                                                                                | 12 axes, held on 22                |
| responses per judge call (batch size B)                      | 7                                                                                                                   | 3 axes, 6 cells, B in 5, 7, 10, 15 |
| fraction of responses judged                                 | tiered: a third, escalating per entity when few responses survive filtering                                         |                                    |
| rubric                                                       | entity anonymised in the response prompt (v2); labels shown in display form (v4); strict score parsing (v5)         | 12 axes                            |
| operating point for ρ                                        | token slot 6, layer 25, soft-shear L=3                                                                              | 35 then 61 axes                    |


The findings behind these, the plots, and the costs are in Appendix A.  The
spend was about $3,400 of judge calls, and the response-mode judging of one
axis on that corpus cost about $52 for GPT and $54 for Haiku.

**What to do now.**  New, cheaper and perhaps better judge models exist, so the
question is the current price/ρ Pareto frontier for description-and-instruction
judging and, separately, for response judging.  Three levers, detailed in
Appendix B:

- (a) the models: an Anthropic model after Haiku 4.5, an OpenAI model after
GPT-4.1-mini, possibly a third from Google;
- (b) for response judging, what fraction of the responses is judged and
averaged over, and the batch size;
- (c) for models that cannot run at temperature 0, several calls per item
averaged.

**What you have.**  The code, documents and small records are in git.  The two
large things are in the Drive folder as archives: the 8slot vectors and
responses, and the per-axis judge caches, 3.9 GB and 3.3 GB unpacked.
Appendix C says what each file is for and how to put the archives in place.
One thing to know before any judging: the corpus text has been rewritten since
May, so judging against the 8slot data must read the corpus as it was then,
from git commit `1448a84c`, through `--instructions_dir` (Appendix C.4).

**Rules that bind every run.**  They are in [AGENT_NOTES.md](../../AGENT_NOTES.md)
at the top; the ones you will meet first:

## Appendix A: what was done



### A.1 The instrument

[axis_judge_correlation.py](../../results_analysis/axis_judge_correlation.py)
is the judge.  Given a pair of entities as poles, it builds a rubric header
from the poles' descriptions, has the judge score every entity in the corpus
in one or more modes, projects every entity's vector onto the pole-difference
direction, and reports Spearman ρ per token slot, raw and whitened.  Its
options that matter here:


| option                                                                       | what it does                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--pair POS NEG --pair_type traits` or `roles` | the axis, from two corpus entities |
| `--data_dir`                                                                 | the extraction tree with `{traits,roles}/vectors/*.pt`                                                                                                                                          |
| `--instructions_dir`                                                         | the corpus tree with `{traits,roles}/instructions/*.json`, default `data`; the entity descriptions and instructions it scores, and the poles' descriptions in the prompt header, come from here |
| `--score_descriptions`, `--score_instructions`, `--score_responses`          | the three modes; `--all` for all three                                                                                                                                                          |
| `--responses_dir`, `--scores_dir`                                            | for response mode: the extraction's responses and the pipeline's per-response trait scores, from which it takes the responses that scored 3                                                     |
| `--response_target_batch_size`                                               | responses per judge call, B, default 7                                                                                                                                                          |
| `--tiered_modulo_per_chunk`, `--question_subsample_modulo`, `--no_subsample` | which fraction of an entity's responses is judged (A.3, item 6)                                                                                                                                 |
| `--provider anthropic` or `openai`, `--judge_model`, `--temperature` (default 0) | the judge |
| `--budget_usd`, `--expected_cost_usd`, `--usage_json`                        | the hard spend cap and the usage record                                                                                                                                                         |
| `--output_dir`                                                               | the cell directory; caches resume on rerun                                                                                                                                                      |


Each run writes a *cell*: `scores_<mode>.json` (the judge scores, and for
responses every batch's reply), `projections.json`, `correlations.json`,
`correlation_plot.png`, `config.json` (the arguments), `usage.json`, `run.log`.
Cells are named by judge family and mode, for example `gpt/`, `sonnet/`,
`gpt_responses_traits_b7/`, `haiku_responses_roles_b7_t3/`.  On a rerun the
cell resumes: entities already scored are kept, and the rubric version and the
judge model recorded in the cache are checked, but the corpus text is not
(hence the corpus pin in C.4).
[run_axis_experiment_batch.py](../../results_analysis/run_axis_experiment_batch.py)
runs the judge over a pair list, several axes in parallel, and writes
[summary.json](../../roger/axis_judge_experiments/summary.json) and
[summary.csv](../../roger/axis_judge_experiments/summary.csv).

Projections are read at a token slot and a layer and, by default, in a
whitened metric: the top principal components of the entity cloud are scaled
down ("soft-K"), or the goal and non-goal subspaces are sheared ("soft-shear
L").  The operating point for every tuning below is slot 6, layer 25,
soft-shear L=3, chosen by the K and L sweeps in A.3, item 8.  Judge scores are
reduced to one number per entity by the constants in
[judge_score_combine.py](../../assistant_axis/judge_score_combine.py).

### A.4 Costs

The cost model was measured by token-counting real prompts
([dry_run_response_token_count.py](../../tools/dry_run_response_token_count.py))
and is in the [judge-cost rule](../../.claude/rules/judge-cost.md) and the
README section "Cost model: GPT-4.1-mini responses-mode judging".  The shape
to remember: a response batch costs about 320 tokens of header plus 438 tokens
per response, and the judge's reply about 80 tokens, so the per-response
content dominates and batch size changes the cost only through the header.
One axis of the 8slot corpus, both kinds, every filtered response:


| judge, regime                             | per axis                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| GPT-4.1-mini, B=7, all responses          | $52                                    |
| Haiku 4.5, B=7, all responses             | $155                                   |
| Haiku 4.5, B=7, a third of the questions  | $52, measured $54 with tier escalation |
| Sonnet 4, B=7, a third                    | $155                                   |
| GPT-4.1-mini, descriptions + instructions | about $1                               |
| Sonnet 4, descriptions + instructions     | about $5                               |


Prices then: GPT-4.1-mini $0.40 in, $1.60 out per million tokens; Haiku 4.5
$1.00 and $5.00; Sonnet 4 $3.00 and $15.00.  Anthropic's tokenizer counts
about 9% more input tokens than OpenAI's on these prompts and Haiku writes
about 2.2 times the output tokens; those scale factors sit in
[plot_batch_size_quality_vs_cost.py](../../results_analysis/plot_batch_size_quality_vs_cost.py).
Roles' responses are about 22% longer than traits'.  Everything scales with
the number of entities times responses per entity: the October extraction's
corpus, 932 traits and 340 roles, makes a response-mode axis about 2.2 times
the figures above.

### A.5 Rubric and parser history

`RUBRIC_VERSION` in the judge: v1 the original; v2, May 2026, the entity's
name hidden in the response prompt; v3, entity names rendered with spaces
instead of underscores; v4, 2026-10-09, labels shown in the judge display form
(a trait imported from an instrument reads "careless (from HEXACO)"); v5,
2026-10-12, the parser requires exactly one `SCORE:` line.  The May caches are
v2 and v3; v4 changed the prompts only of entities whose display label is not
the stem with spaces, which in the 8slot corpus is 11 entities, ten
hyphenated or multi-word traits such as
[stream_of_consciousness](../../data/traits/instructions/stream_of_consciousness.json)
and the role [devils_advocate](../../data/roles/instructions/devils_advocate.json);
v5 changed only parsing, with every recorded reply re-parsed.  Equivalences between versions
are declared in [rubric_equivalences.yaml](../../rubric_equivalences.yaml) and
audited by [audit_rubric_versions.py](../../tools/audit_rubric_versions.py).

### A.6 What transfers and what does not

The *decisions* transfer: they are code defaults and the recipe for the next
re-score.  The *caches* are valid only against the 8slot data and the May
corpus: instructions were rewritten for nearly every entity since, a pole's
description changed on 26 of the 61 axes and 9 of the 22 response axes, and
the responses themselves are those of the old instructions.  Nothing in them
can be reused for the October extraction, whose corpus is also more than twice
the size.  For the Pareto work on the 8slot data they are exactly what is
needed: a new judge is compared on the same response batches the old ones
scored.

Two things have moved under the judge choice since May.  Claude Sonnet 4 is
retired and the Anthropic default is Sonnet 4.6, so the first touch of a
Sonnet cell rejudges it whole, about $4 to $6 an axis (see "Judge-model drift
on resume" in the [judging rule](../../.claude/rules/judging.md)).  And the
cheaper 5.x models run at temperature 1 only (B.2, B.4).

### A.7 Provenance

Every JSON record and PNG an analysis script writes carries a list of its
inputs with fingerprints, and two tools classify them as current, stale,
deferred with a recorded reason, frozen, or legacy:

```bash
uv run python tools/audit_pngs.py   --root roger/axis_judge_experiments --format flat
uv run python tools/audit_caches.py --root roger/axis_judge_experiments --format flat
```

The fingerprints are modification time and size, so files must be copied
with their mtimes (the archives do this).  The May judge caches are deferred
as "awaiting the October re-score"; the May 35-axis desc+inst sweeps are
frozen as the record behind the L=3 decision; the rest of what is tracked
audits current.  The registry of deferrals is
[deferred_rejudges.yaml](../../deferred_rejudges.yaml), and the review that
set the current state is
[regeneration_list.md](../code_review_2026-10-12/regeneration_list.md).  A
plot you add to git must carry its inputs (the writer pattern in the
[provenance rule](../../.claude/rules/provenance.md)) and audit current, or be
deferred with a reason.

### A.8 Things learned the hard way

- A run was launched at B=10 after the default had moved to B=7, $364 sunk;
hence the confirm-over-$20 rule.
- A GPT judge run had a 17 to 31% unparseable rate that nobody noticed for
days; hence the mandatory parse-rate warning below 99%.
- Reducing the Haiku loader's fallback from `(7, 10)` to `(7,)` silently
dropped most of the original 12 axes' coverage; the fallback stays.
- Eleven names are both a trait and a role; every mixed-kind structure keys on
`name|T` / `name|R` ([entity-naming rule](../../.claude/rules/entity-naming.md)).
- Caches are keyed by judge family, not model id; a model change inside a
family is caught on resume only because the recorded model is compared.
- Sonnet 5.5 refuses a prompt that asks it to think in a JSON field; ask for a
short reason before the score, never for its reasoning.
- A judge that sees two candidates in one prompt favours a slot; rate items
alone, or swap and average.
- Before overwriting any expensive cache, copy it with a timestamp.

## Appendix B: the price/ρ frontier investigation



### B.1 Framing

Two frontiers, measured the same way: for a judge configuration, its cost per
axis (from the token counts and prices, confirmed by the `usage.json` of the
run) against the mean ρ it reaches over a cohort of axes at the operating
point.  The existing points are GPT-4.1-mini and Haiku 4.5 at their chosen
settings, and the May records give their ρ on every axis.  Because the 8slot
responses and the old judges' per-batch scores are on disk, a new judge can
score the *same batches* and be compared entity by entity and axis by axis,
which is far more sensitive than comparing cohort means.
[plot_batch_size_quality_vs_cost.py](../../results_analysis/plot_batch_size_quality_vs_cost.py)
draws such fronts, with quality 1/(1−ρ) so that gains near the ceiling show.

Do the pilot on the three axes of the batch-size study,
[truthful](../../data/traits/instructions/truthful.json) / [deceitful](../../data/traits/instructions/deceitful.json),
[progressive](../../data/traits/instructions/progressive.json) / [conservative](../../data/traits/instructions/conservative.json)
and [improvisational](../../data/traits/instructions/improvisational.json) / [methodical](../../data/traits/instructions/methodical.json),
which have the densest existing coverage, then widen to the 22-axis response
cohort for the response frontier and the 61-axis cohort for the
description-and-instruction frontier.  Description-and-instruction judging is
cheap enough to run every candidate judge on all 61 axes.

### B.2 Lever (a): the models

**Anthropic, after Haiku 4.5.**  Haiku 5.5 is priced in
[judge_pricing.py](../../assistant_axis/judge_pricing.py) at $0.10 and $0.50
per million tokens, a tenth of Haiku 4.5; Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10, Opus 5.5
at $4 and $20.  All 5.x models refuse any temperature but the default of 1
(the capability table `MODEL_CAPABILITIES` in
[judge.py](../../assistant_axis/judge.py) makes the judge raise before the
first call), so they bring lever (c) with them.  The project already uses
Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 as judges elsewhere (the trait-gap platform), at
temperature 1 with three readings and a vote.

**OpenAI, after GPT-4.1-mini.**  GPT-4.1-mini is still served (checked
2026-10-10) and remains the pipeline's own judge.  The capability table lists
the `gpt-5` and `gpt-6` families as refusing a temperature; no rate rows exist
for them yet; and the judge sends no reasoning-effort setting, which a
reasoning model needs or it bills its reasoning at the default effort.  Pick
the candidate from OpenAI's current small models, verify the id with
`client.models.list()`, add the rate row and the capability row, and add the
reasoning-effort parameter to the call path before the first run.

**Google.**  No client exists in the repository; `--provider` takes
`anthropic` or `openai`.  A Gemini arm needs a client in
[judge.py](../../assistant_axis/judge.py) alongside the other two, a rate row,
a capability row, usage extraction for the pricing tracker, and a key in
`.env`.  The rule is a direct call with Google's own key, not a router.

**What a comparison needs on disk.**  Cells are named by judge family, and
the resume check compares the recorded model with the current one, so keep
each candidate in its own directory: the batch driver's `--subdir`, or
`--output_dir` on the judge, with a name such as `haiku55_responses_traits_b7_t3`.
The loaders and sweeps take the judge name from the directory, so a new family
name is also a loader change in [judge_loaders.py](../../assistant_axis/judge_loaders.py)
and the sweep scripts; the simplest route is to write the candidate's cells
with the batch driver and read them with a short script modelled on
[gpt_anthropic_response_weight_sweep.py](../../results_analysis/gpt_anthropic_response_weight_sweep.py).
For every new model, measure its tokenizer and verbosity scale factors from the
pilot's `usage.json` before quoting a full-cohort cost.

### B.3 Lever (b): how much of the response set is judged

Two knobs, both on the judge and the driver: the batch size
`--response_target_batch_size`, and the subsample, either tiered
(`--tiered_modulo_per_chunk M`, default 3) or uniform
(`--question_subsample_modulo N`), or none (`--no_subsample`).  Cost is close
to linear in the fraction judged, and the May curve says the batch size moves
ρ by about 0.01 across its whole range.  What was never measured is ρ against
the fraction at fixed B: the tiered third was adopted on cost grounds, and the
only evidence is that Haiku on a third of the questions matched GPT on all of
them within the ensemble.  A grid of fraction in {a third, two thirds, all}
by B in {5, 7, 10} on the pilot axes, for the cheapest candidate judge, gives
that curve for about the cost of two full-volume axes.  Keep the cell suffixes
consistent with the loader's conventions, `_b{B}` and `_t{M}` or `_q{N}`, so
the readers find them.  Note the asymmetry the May work settled on: GPT at
full volume because it is cheap, the Anthropic judge subsampled; a tenfold
cheaper Haiku changes that arithmetic.

### B.4 Lever (c): several samples per item at temperature 1

The axis judge has no such option.  The pattern exists in
[score_combinations.py](../../data_analysis/score_combinations.py)
(`--rescore_samples`, the cache keyed on the sample index, scores combined by
median) and [classify_goals.py](../../data_analysis/classify_goals.py)
(`--readings`).  For the judge: a `--samples N` that keys each batch's reply
on its sample index, averages the N batch scores, and records every reply, so
that the single-sample ρ and the N-sample ρ come from the same run.  Cost is
N times.  Roger's rules for sampled outputs: never gate on agreement between
samples or discard the disagreeing ones; combine them; and average several
samples only when the price gap makes it worth it, which at a tenth of the
price it does, since three samples of Haiku 5.5 cost a third of one sample of
Haiku 4.5.  The interesting measurement is ρ at N = 1, 2, 3 against cost, on
the pilot axes, in both modes.

### B.5 Order of work and budgets

1. A smoke run of each candidate judge on one axis's descriptions, cents, to
  settle the request shape, the parse rate and the capability rows.
2. The description-and-instruction frontier on all 61 axes for every
  candidate: a few dollars per judge.
3. The response pilot on the three axes: every candidate at B=7 on the tiered
  third, and lever (c) at N up to 3 for the T=1 models; then lever (b)'s grid
   for the best cheap candidate.  Quote the plan to Roger before anything over
   $20, batched where the run is large.
4. The response frontier on the 22-axis cohort for the two or three
  configurations the pilot leaves standing.
5. If a judge is adopted, the re-tuning checklist in the README section
  "Convention: tuned mixing ratios" (sweeps 2 and 3) and the
   [add-judged-axis skill](../../.claude/skills/add-judged-axis/SKILL.md).

Every run records its usage; every result goes in a provenance envelope;
every write-up links the files it names.

## Appendix D: glossary

- **entity**: a trait or a role in the corpus; **stem**: its file name, such as
`systems_thinker`; **label**: its display name.
- **axis**: a direction in activation space; here the difference of two
entities' vectors, positive pole minus negative pole.
- **projection**: an entity's vector dotted with the axis direction, at one
token slot and layer, in the raw or a whitened metric.
- **ρ**: Spearman rank correlation between judge scores and projections over
the corpus; the quality measure.
- **slot**: one of eight token positions in the chat-template header whose
activations were saved; slot 6 is the `</think>` token.  **layer**: one of
64 transformer layers; 25 is the operating layer.
- **soft-K**: scaling the top K principal components of the entity cloud down
to the (K+1)th; **soft-shear L**: shearing the goal and non-goal subspaces
of the first L canonical pairs; **operating point**: slot 6, layer 25,
soft-shear L=3.
- **desc, inst, di**: description mode, instruction mode, and their blend.
- **response mode**: judging batches of the model's responses; **B**: responses
per judge call; **t3**: tiered subsampling with chunk size 3; **q9**: the
legacy uniform ninth of the questions.
- **cell**: one judge run's output directory for one axis, judge and mode.
- **cohort**: a set of axes a sweep averages over; named by its pair list.
- **rubric version**: `RUBRIC_VERSION` in the judge, v1 to v5 (A.5).
- **goal / non-goal**: entities whose persona has an aim of its own versus
not; the split the shear whitening uses.
- **RP**: role-play filtering, the pipeline step that keeps responses in which
the model stays in persona.
- **provenance envelope**: the `_provenance` block in a record, or the
`Inputs` chunk in a PNG, listing the inputs and their fingerprints.
