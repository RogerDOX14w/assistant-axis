>
>    - **Strong version (Linguistic Determinism):** Language determines thought. According to this view, people can only think about what their language allows them to express.  
>    - **Weak version (Linguistic Relativity):** Language influences thought, but does not determine it. This version is more widely accepted today and suggests that language can shape certain aspects of cognition, such as perception and categorization.
>
>    For example, some languages have more specific words for colors or spatial relationships than others, and this may influence how speakers of those languages categorize or remember visual information.
>
> 2. **The Language of Thought Hypothesis (Mentalism):**  
>    Proposed by philosophers like Jerry Fodor, this theory posits that thought occurs in a "language of thought" (often abbreviated as "mentalese"), which is a system of mental representations that operates independently of spoken or written language. According to this view, language is a tool for expressing thoughts, not the medium in which thoughts are formed.
>
> 3. **The Innate vs. Learned Debate:**  
>    - **Noam Chomsky** argued that humans have an innate capacity for language (the "language acquisition device"), suggesting that the structure of language is hardwired in the brain and that thought and language are closely linked in development.

>
> 4. **Empirical Studies on Language and Thought:**  
>    Modern cognitive science and neuroscience have provided evidence that language and thought are closely intertwined but not identical. For example:
