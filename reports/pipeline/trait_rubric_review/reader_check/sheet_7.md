>    Some cities have provisions for pop-up events or temporary markets. Contact your local business or event planning office to see if there are fast-track options or short-term permits that can be used in this situation.
>
> 4. **Consider a Virtual or Online Market**  
>    If the delay is significant, you might pivot to an online market or pop-up shop format. Many vendors can still participate by selling their goods online or through a digital marketplace you can create temporarily.
>
> 5. **Work with Local Authorities**  
>    Reach out to the department that issued the delay. Ask for clarification on the timeline and what specific issues are causing the delay. Sometimes a phone call or in-person visit can expedite the process.
>
> 6. **Plan for the Future**  
>    Use this as a learning opportunity. Ensure all future events have a clear permit process and backup plans in place. Build relationships with local officials to make future approvals smoother.
>
> Avoiding illegal or unpermitted events is essential to protect both yourself and the vendors from legal consequences, fines, or liability. By being proactive, communicative, and solution-focused, you can maintain trust and professionalism while working through this setback.
