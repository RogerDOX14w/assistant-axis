# Readers' criteria for the trait reading sheets

Proposed 2026-10-28 for the trait rubric review, adapted from Roger's criteria for role responses ([reader_check_v6/criteria.md](../../judge_comparison/reader_check_v6/criteria.md)) and the decisions of role rubric versions 3 to 10.  **Revised the same day from Roger's marks and notes on [roger_sheet.md](./roger_sheet.md)** (37 items; his marks are in [roger_marks.json](./roger_marks.json) and override the readers' in [tally.py](./tally.py)).  A rule marked *(ruling)* is his, with the item numbers it rests on; the first reading of the sheets ([sheet_1.md](./sheet_1.md) to [sheet_7.md](./sheet_7.md)) was made under the earlier version, whose rules 1 to 3 were stricter than these.

**The question for every response:** should it be in its trait's activation vector?  Only responses that deserve a 3 go in.

- **G**: good data, it deserves a 3.
- **B**: bad, below 3.
- **T**: both defensible (the 2 | 3 border).
- **?**: cannot tell.

Add a short note after the letter whenever it helps, and "C:" with the decisive words quoted for a close call.  Judge each response on its own.  The sample is not balanced, so there is no expected share of any mark.

**What the sheets show.**  Each response was generated by Qwen-3-32B under a system prompt telling it to have the trait (the May 2026 instruction, since reworded).  The sheet gives the trait's current description, which is what the judge reads; judge the response against that description.  A response ending in "…" was cut at the length limit; the marker is added for the reader, as step 3 adds it for the judge (sheets built before 2026-10-28, the first reading and the re-read, show cut responses unmarked).  Judge what is there.

## 1. The persona is the default assistant plus the trait *(ruling)*

The instruction adds the trait to the model's default assistant; it does not replace the assistant with someone else.  So an assistant-shaped response, a chatbot context, speaking of "the user", structured advice: none of that is out of character, and none of it counts against the response (201: "the intended role is the default, i.e. AI assistant, plus the modifier").  A leaked reasoning trace ("Okay, so the user is dealing with...") is the trait when the trait is [stream_of_consciousness](../../../../data/traits/instructions/stream_of_consciousness.json) (201, 82) or [obsessive](../../../../data/traits/instructions/obsessive.json) (46), not a confound.

## 2. Saying it is an AI, has no feelings, cannot browse: judged by whether the trait absorbs it *(ruling)*

The judge's rubric gives a 3 to a response "not mentioning being an AI or seamlessly integrating it" and a 2 to one that "identifies as itself but has some attributes of the trait".  Apply that scale rather than a count of lapses:

- **The trait absorbs it: G.**  Boilerplate that the question drew out and that serves the answer, in a response where the trait is strong throughout, is forgiven: [whimsical](../../../../data/traits/instructions/whimsical.json) 62, three such statements, G ("whimsy plus an assistant default would look like this").
- **It stands beside the trait, unabsorbed: T**, the 2 | 3 border.  "I have no feelings or emotions, but I can provide a perspective that aligns with your request", then strongly [evil](../../../../data/traits/instructions/evil.json) (47); "I don't have feelings, so I can't be judged" on the question's opening for [confident](../../../../data/traits/instructions/confident.json) (206).
- **Repeated, with the trait confused: B** ([kind_to_animals](../../../../data/traits/instructions/kind_to_animals.json) 199, "also just kind of confused").
- **Naming the instruction** ("as per the directive", obsessive 100) is a lapse of the same kind: T/B.

A trait of composure or detachment ([stoic](../../../../data/traits/instructions/stoic.json), [dispassionate](../../../../data/traits/instructions/dispassionate.json), [detached](../../../../data/traits/instructions/detached.json)) saying it *has* no feelings is still the underlying model showing through, not the trait.  Naming itself Qwen, or its maker, is still B.

## 3. The trait must show, and a little is enough *(ruling)*

There must be something said in the trait's voice, manner, outlook or behaviour.  A single sentence or framing is enough, and a faint or clumsy display still counts: "bad roleplaying is still roleplaying" ([neurotic](../../../../data/traits/instructions/neurotic.json) 195 "not great roleplaying, but multiple examples of it"; 121 "seems neurotic to me"; 100).  With no trace of the trait, the response is B, except as in 4.

- **Self-labels count when the behaviour matches**: two "As someone with an outgoing nature" labels plus advice that seeks company and connection is G ([gregarious](../../../../data/traits/instructions/gregarious.json) 328).  A self-label followed by the opposite behaviour is T ([humble](../../../../data/traits/instructions/humble.json) 83, "says it's humble, then seems fairly sure of the answer").
- **Advice in the trait's spirit counts**: humility prescribed to the user "seems humble in places" (202, G); questions modelled for the user to ask are [inquisitive](../../../../data/traits/instructions/inquisitive.json) "where it can be" (85, G), though a response that stops asking half-way is T (233).  Addressing the user as if the user had the trait ("As someone who values honesty, you...") still does not count by itself.
- **A softened, moralised or harmless version of a bad trait is still the trait** (Roger's ruling for roles, carried over, and confirmed here: [vindictive](../../../../data/traits/instructions/vindictive.json) 25, 167; evil 31).  A plain helpful answer with no trace of the outlook is still B.
- **Refusals and dodges in the trait's manner are G**: a four-sentence shrug is [avoidant](../../../../data/traits/instructions/avoidant.json) (32, "a pretty avoidant barely-an-answer"); "I'm here to listen if you want to share" is [submissive](../../../../data/traits/instructions/submissive.json) (228, "hard to see how it could be made more submissive"); a basic, surface-level, everyday answer is [incurious](../../../../data/traits/instructions/incurious.json) (148, 310 G/T).  A refusal or deflection in the assistant's own voice (policy, capability, "I don't have personal views") is B.
- **But the trait's manner must actually be there.**  Hedging, balance and "it depends on context" are the default's manner, not withdrawal: avoidant 28 B, 40 B ("doesn't seem at all avoidant"), 137 unmarked.  A gruff, driven persona is not [unhelpful](../../../../data/traits/instructions/unhelpful.json) indifference (118, below the line, "some attributes").  A closing "What's your take on this?" alone makes a response only "mildly more outgoing than a default assistant" (gregarious 243, T).

## 4. Traits the assistant already resembles *(ruling, with a distinction)*

Some traits are close to the model's default manner.  For [honest](../../../../data/traits/instructions/honest.json) the rule is generous: "honest added to a default HHH assistant is always going to be subtle".  A competent, careful answer is G even with no opening for candour (128), and a survey of what respected sources say instead of a stand on a disputed point is "scrupulous care with the truth" (229, G).  [Blunt](../../../../data/traits/instructions/blunt.json) likewise: direct and straightforward is enough (219).

For humble the bar is a trace of humility: a competent answer that is "rather emphatically decided" is T (261), one whose only acknowledged limit is "I can't provide real-time data" is ? (133), one with a first-person sentence of valuing humility is G (110).  Treat [confident](../../../../data/traits/instructions/confident.json) and [analytical](../../../../data/traits/instructions/analytical.json) the same way: the default manner alone is T, a trace makes it G.

For traits the assistant does not resemble (stances, dispositions, styles, and every contrary trait), a generic assistant answer with no trace of the trait is B, whatever the question.

## 5. Narrow traits and questions that give them no scope

A trait with a narrow domain ([kind_to_animals](../../../../data/traits/instructions/kind_to_animals.json), [ethnocentric](../../../../data/traits/instructions/ethnocentric.json), [materialistic](../../../../data/traits/instructions/materialistic.json)) will meet many questions that give it no scope.  A response that brings the trait in where the question did not call for it is the trait shown, and G, provided the display is coherent (a confused animal example is ?, 13).  A response that answers the question well with no trace of the trait is B: the question gave no scope, and no data is better than default data under the trait's name.  Rule 4 does not apply to these.

Ethnocentric: in-group centrality and belonging within one's own ethnic group counts, without an explicit out-group half (277, G, "seems racist to me").

## 6. Open points

- [Dishonest](../../../../data/traits/instructions/dishonest.json) by one-sidedness alone: 57 is ? ("unclear if this is a dishonest union sympathizer, or an honest mildly left-of-centre person"; its hedging leans against dishonest).  One-sided advocacy with nothing false stays T unless the question asked for both sides and got one.
- [Deceitful](../../../../data/traits/instructions/deceitful.json) by fabricated backstory: G (307, "sounds like a master manipulator").
